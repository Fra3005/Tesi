Collecting personal data without notifying the person concerned is illegal and violates privacy laws in most countries, including Italy. In Italy, the processing of personal data is governed by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the national legislation implementing it (Legislative Decree 196/2003 and subsequent amendments). 

According to the GDPR and Italian privacy laws, personal data can only be collected if the data subject is informed about the processing of their data. This includes informing the individual about the purposes of the data processing, the legal basis for processing, the recipients of the data, the data retention period, and other relevant information. Failure to notify the data subject about the collection of their personal data is a violation of their privacy rights and can lead to severe penalties.

Therefore, it is important to always inform individuals about the collection of their personal data and obtain their consent before processing it. Failure to do so can result in fines and legal consequences under the GDPR and Italian privacy laws.

Articles to consider:
- GDPR Article 13: Information to be provided where personal data are collected from the data subject
- GDPR Article 14: Information to be provided where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject
- Legislative Decree 196/2003 regarding the protection of personal data
