1. Gift Tax Exclusion: In the United States, there is an annual gift tax exclusion which allows you to gift up to a certain amount (currently $15,000 per person in 2021) without having to pay gift taxes. This means you can gift the house to your child up to the annual exclusion amount without incurring gift tax.

2. Lifetime Gift Tax Exemption: In addition to the annual gift tax exclusion, there is a lifetime gift tax exemption (currently $11.7 million per person in 2021) which allows you to gift a total amount over your lifetime without paying gift taxes. If the value of the house exceeds the annual exclusion amount, you may utilize your lifetime gift tax exemption to avoid gift taxes.

3. Consider a Qualified Personal Residence Trust (QPRT): A QPRT is a trust that allows you to transfer ownership of your home to your child while retaining the right to live in the home for a specified period. This can potentially reduce the value of the gift for tax purposes.

4. Consult with a Tax Professional: It is advisable to consult with a tax professional or estate planning attorney to discuss your specific situation and explore all available options to minimize taxes when gifting a house to your child.

Note: It is important to be aware of the relevant tax laws in your jurisdiction and seek professional advice to ensure compliance with all legal requirements. Failure to do so may result in penalties or tax liabilities.
