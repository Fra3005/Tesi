
=== Answer (Risposta: 1) ===
It is not legal to collect personal data without notifying the person concerned and obtaining their consent, as it would violate privacy laws and data protection regulations. These laws aim to protect the privacy rights of individuals and ensure that their personal data is processed fairly, lawfully, and transparently.

Violations in this context may include:

1. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (EU) 2016/679:
   - Article 5(1)(a): Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner.
   - Article 12: The data controller must provide clear and concise information to the data subject at the time of data collection, including the identity of the controller, the purpose of processing, and the legal basis for processing.
   - Article 13: When personal data is collected directly from a data subject, the data controller must provide them with specific information about the processing and their rights.

2. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.):
   - Section 1798.100(b): A business must inform consumers, at or before the point of collection, of the categories of personal information to be collected and the purposes for which the categories of personal information shall be used.
   - Section 1798.115(a): A business shall not collect personal information from consumers without providing notice.

3. Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) (Canada):
   - Principle 4.3: A knowledgeable understanding of the purposes for which personal information is being collected must be obtained prior to or at the time of collection.
   - Principle 4.8: Individuals shall be informed of the existence, use, and disclosure of their personal information and shall be given access to that information.

Please ensure that you comply with applicable data protection regulations when collecting personal data to avoid legal consequences.

=== Answer (Risposta: 5) ===
It is not legal to collect personal data without notifying the person concerned in most jurisdictions, including the European Union, United States, and many other countries. Notifying the data subject is a fundamental principle of data protection laws, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the EU and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States. These laws require data controllers to be transparent about their data processing activities and inform data subjects about the personal data being collected, the purpose of the collection, and the rights of the data subjects.

Violating data protection laws can result in significant fines and penalties. For example, under the GDPR, organizations can be fined up to 4% of their global annual revenue or €20 million (whichever is greater) for non-compliance.

Therefore, it is essential to comply with data protection laws and notify data subjects before collecting their personal data, except in specific situations where the law allows for exceptions, such as:

* The data subject has made the personal data publicly available.
* The collection is necessary for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims.
* The collection is required by law or regulation.

Even in these exceptions, data controllers must still comply with other data protection principles, such as minimizing the amount of data collected, ensuring the data is accurate, and securing the data against unauthorized access or disclosure.
