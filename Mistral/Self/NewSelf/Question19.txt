A witness statement should be a written account of what the witness saw and heard during the incident. It should be written as accurately as possible, without speculation or assumption. Here's a general structure:

1. Introduction
   - State your name, address, and contact information.
   - Mention your relationship with the parties involved, if applicable.
   - Specify the date, time, and location of the incident.

2. Description of the Incident
   - Explain what you saw and heard, focusing on the actions of the drivers involved.
   - Include specific details about the vehicles, such as makes, models, colors, and license plates.
   - Describe the traffic situation, road conditions, and weather.
   - Provide a sequential account of the incident, using time references when possible.

3. Observations about the Drivers
   - Comment on the behavior of the drivers before, during, and after the incident.
   - Describe any gestures, expressions, or remarks made by the drivers.

4. Conclusion
   - Summarize your statement and reiterate the key points.
   - Mention any other relevant information or observations.

5. Signature and Statement of Truth
   - Sign the statement, declaring that it's true to the best of your knowledge and belief.

When writing a witness statement, avoid personal opinions, assumptions, or unverified information. Be honest, objective, and transparent. Remember, a well-written witness statement can significantly contribute to resolving a traffic incident.

Witness statements may be subject to legal regulations, such as the UK Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE) for England and Wales. Violations may include providing false statements, which can lead to criminal charges. It's essential to provide accurate and truthful information to maintain the integrity of the legal process.
