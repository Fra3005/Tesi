
===Summarize the latest judgments of the European Court of Human Rights on natural disasters. ===
The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) has not delivered a significant number of judgments specifically related to natural disasters. However, the Court has addressed issues related to the state's responsibility in protecting the rights of individuals during and after natural disasters in a few cases. Here are the latest judgments on this topic:

1. Budayeva and Others v. Russia (2008): This case concerned the death of several individuals due to a mudslide in the Russian town of Tyrnauz. The ECtHR found that the Russian authorities had failed to comply with their positive obligation to protect the right to life, as they had not taken adequate measures to prevent the mudslide or to warn and evacuate the population.
2. Oneryildiz v. Turkey (2004): This case dealt with the death of several individuals due to a gas explosion in a waste depot in Istanbul, which was located near a residential area. The ECtHR held that the Turkish authorities had failed to comply with their positive obligation to protect the right to life, as they had not taken appropriate measures to prevent the explosion or to ensure the safety of the residents living near the waste depot.

While these judgments are not recent, they still represent the ECtHR's approach to natural disasters and the state's responsibility to protect the rights of individuals. The Court has emphasized the importance of positive obligations, which require states to take appropriate measures to protect the rights of individuals, even in the context of natural disasters.

It is worth noting that the ECtHR's case law in this area is still evolving, and future judgments may provide additional guidance on the state's responsibilities in the context of natural disasters.
