Collecting personal data without notifying the person concerned is generally not allowed under European data protection law, specifically the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The GDPR is a regulation in EU law that focuses on data protection and privacy in the European Union and the European Economic Area. It also addresses the transfer of personal data outside these regions.

The GDPR states that personal data should be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner. This means that individuals have the right to know when their personal data is being collected, how it will be used, and why. Therefore, collecting personal data without notifying the person concerned would be a violation of the GDPR's transparency principle.

There are some exceptions to this rule, such as when personal data is collected for scientific or statistical research purposes, where informing the data subject would be likely to prejudice the objectives of the research. However, in these cases, the data controller must still ensure that appropriate safeguards are in place to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.

In any case, it is essential to consult a legal expert to ensure compliance with the GDPR and any other applicable data protection laws.

In summary, generally, you cannot collect personal data without notifying the person concerned under GDPR. However, there may be specific exceptions for scientific or statistical research purposes, but it is crucial to consult a legal expert and ensure that appropriate safeguards are in place.

Articles related to the GDPR that you may violate if you collect personal data without notifying the person concerned:

- Article 5 (1) (a) of the GDPR: Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner.
- Article 13 of the GDPR: Information to be provided where personal data are collected from the data subject.
- Article 14 of the GDPR: Information to be provided where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject.
- Recital 39 of the GDPR: The principle of transparency requires that any information addressed to the public or to the data subject be concise, easily accessible and easy to understand.
- Recital 159 of the GDPR: The processing of personal data for scientific or statistical research purposes should be carried out under the appropriate safeguards for the rights and freedoms of the data subject.
