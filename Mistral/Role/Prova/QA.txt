Brewing your own gin at home is generally legal in many places, as long as you do not sell it or give it to minors. However, the specific legality can depend on the laws of your particular location, so it is always a good idea to check with your local authorities to ensure that you are in compliance with any relevant regulations.

To brew your own gin, you will need to start with a neutral grain spirit, such as vodka, and then add botanicals, such as juniper berries, coriander, and citrus peel, to flavor the spirit. The specific botanicals and their proportions can be adjusted to taste.

Here is a basic recipe for brewing your own gin:

1. Gather your ingredients:
	* Neutral grain spirit (such as vodka)
	* Botanicals (such as juniper berries, coriander, citrus peel, and any other flavors you want to add)
	* A large, clean glass jar with a lid
	* Cheesecloth or a coffee filter
	* A funnel
2. Add the botanicals to the jar:
	* Add the juniper berries, coriander, citrus peel, and any other botanicals you want to use to the jar.
	* The exact proportions will depend on your personal preference, but a good starting point is to use 1 tablespoon of juniper berries, 1 teaspoon of coriander, and the zest of 1 lemon or orange for every 750 ml of neutral grain spirit.
3. Add the neutral grain spirit to the jar:
	* Pour the vodka (or other neutral grain spirit) over the botanicals in the jar.
	* Make sure that the botanicals are fully submerged in the spirit.
4. Let the mixture steep:
	* Cover the jar with a lid and let the mixture steep for 24-48 hours.
	* You can taste the gin periodically during this time to see how the flavor is developing.
5. Strain the gin:
	* After the gin has steeped for the desired amount of time, strain it through a cheesecloth or coffee filter to remove the solid botanicals.
	* Be sure to squeeze out as much of the liquid as possible.
6. Store the gin:
	* Transfer the strained gin to a clean glass bottle or jar.
	* The gin can be stored at room temperature or in the refrigerator.

Enjoy your homemade gin! Keep in mind that homemade gin may not have the same level of refinement or consistency as commercially produced gin, but it can be a fun and rewarding project to try.
