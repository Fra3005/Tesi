However, for gin production, it's more common to use a pre-made neutral spirit, often referred to as 'gin neutral spirit' or 'GNS', which can be purchased from a supplier.

Once you have your GNS, you can start adding the botanicals that give gin its unique flavor and aroma. Common gin botanicals include juniper berries, coriander seeds, citrus peels, and angelica root. You can use a combination of these botanicals to create your desired flavor profile.

To infuse the botanicals into the GNS, you can use a process called 'cold compounding', where the botanicals are steeped in the GNS for a period of time, usually several days or weeks. Alternatively, you can use a process called 'hot compounding', where the botanicals are heated in the GNS to extract the flavors and oils.

Once the infusion process is complete, you can filter the mixture to remove the botanicals and achieve a clear, spirit. The resulting liquid is your homemade gin.
