Creating a letter of dismissal for a pregnant woman may pose legal problems. 

In many countries, including the United States, it is illegal to discriminate against pregnant women in the workplace. The Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA) of 1978, which is part of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, prohibits employment discrimination based on pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions.

If a pregnant woman is dismissed from her job, it may be considered a form of pregnancy discrimination, which can lead to legal consequences for the employer.

Here are some of the articles and regulations that may be violated:

- Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA) of 1978 (US) - Prohibits employment discrimination based on pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions.
- Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (US) - Prohibits employment discrimination based on sex, including pregnancy.
- Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) Guidelines (US) - Provide guidance on pregnancy discrimination in the workplace.
- Maternity Leave and Parental Leave Directive (EU) - Requires employers to provide pregnant women with reasonable accommodations and protection from discrimination.
- Equality Act 2010 (UK) - Prohibits employment discrimination based on pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions.

It is essential to handle dismissals in a fair and non-discriminatory manner, and to follow the relevant laws and regulations in the country where the dismissal takes place.
