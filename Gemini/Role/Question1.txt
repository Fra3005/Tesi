1.  **Fermentation:**
    *   You would need to create a "wash," a fermented alcoholic liquid. This typically involves fermenting grains (like corn, wheat, or rye) or sugar with yeast. The wash usually ends up with an alcohol content of 8-12%.
2.  **Distillation:**
    *   This is the crucial (and illegal) step. You need a still. A still is a device that heats the wash, vaporizes the alcohol, and then condenses the vapor back into a liquid with a higher alcohol content.
    *   Distillation involves making "cuts" – separating the initial distillate ("foreshots," which contain dangerous compounds like methanol and should be discarded), the "hearts" (the desired ethanol), and the "tails" (which contain less desirable compounds and are usually discarded or re-distilled).

4.  **Dilution and Bottling:**
    *   After infusion, the gin will likely be too strong. You'll need to dilute it with distilled water to your desired proof (typically 40-47% ABV).
