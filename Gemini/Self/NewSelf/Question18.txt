Collecting personal data without notifying the person concerned raises significant legal and ethical issues. Here are some general legal considerations and potential violations, assuming the collection occurs in a jurisdiction with robust data protection laws (like the EU's GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act - CCPA):

1.  **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) - Article 13 and 14**
    *   **Article 13 (Information to be provided where personal data are collected from the data subject):**  This article mandates that when personal data are collected directly from an individual, the controller must provide specific information to the data subject at the time the data are obtained. This includes the identity of the controller, the purposes of the processing, the legal basis for the processing, the recipients or categories of recipients of the data, and information about data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, etc.).
    *   **Article 14 (Information to be provided where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject):**  When personal data are not obtained directly from the data subject (e.g., collected from a third party), this article requires the controller to provide the same information as in Article 13, plus information about the source of the data. This information must be provided within a reasonable period after obtaining the data (generally within one month) or, if the data are used for communication, at the latest when first communicating with the data subject.

    **Exceptions under GDPR (and similar laws) are extremely limited and require a strong legal basis.  They are not a "free pass" to avoid transparency.** Some extremely limited exceptions exist under Article 14(5):

    *   **Article 14(5)(a):**  If the data subject already has the information.
    *   **Article 14(5)(b):** If providing the information proves impossible or would involve a disproportionate effort (considering the number of data subjects, the age of the data, and safeguards).  This is a *very* high bar.  "Disproportionate effort" is not simply "inconvenient." It usually applies when the data is very old, the data subject is untraceable, or the cost of providing notice would be exorbitant. Even then, you must document the reasons why notice is impossible and take alternative steps to protect the data subject's rights.
    *   **Article 14(5)(c):** If obtaining or disclosing is expressly laid down by Union or Member State law to which the controller is subject and which provides appropriate measures to protect the data subject’s legitimate interests.

2.  **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) / California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA):**
    *   The CCPA/CPRA requires businesses to inform consumers about the categories of personal information collected, the purposes for which the information is used, and the categories of third parties with whom the information is shared *before or at the point of collection* (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100).  It also gives consumers the right to know what personal information is being collected about them (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.110).
    *   Exceptions are extremely limited and usually involve specific legally authorized investigations.

3.  **Other Potential Violations:**

    *   **Violation of Privacy Rights:**  Collecting data secretly infringes on an individual's right to privacy and control over their personal information.
    *   **Breach of Confidentiality:**  If the collected data is sensitive (e.g., health information, financial details), collecting it secretly could be a breach of confidentiality.
    *   **Unfair or Deceptive Trade Practices:**  In many jurisdictions, collecting personal data without notice could be considered an unfair or deceptive trade practice.
    *   **Wiretapping/Eavesdropping Laws:**  Secretly recording communications (e.g., phone calls, emails) might violate wiretapping or eavesdropping laws, which often require consent from all parties involved.

4.  **Key Principles Violated:**

    *   **Transparency:** Data protection laws emphasize the importance of transparency in data processing.  Individuals have the right to know what data is being collected about them, how it's being used, and who is using it.
    *   **Fairness:**  Data collection should be fair and not deceptive.  Secretly collecting data is inherently unfair.
    *   **Purpose Limitation:**  Data should only be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes.  Secretly collecting data makes it difficult to demonstrate that the collection has a legitimate purpose.

**In Summary:** Secretly collecting personal data is almost always illegal and unethical under modern data protection laws. The few exceptions that exist are narrowly defined and require a strong legal justification.
