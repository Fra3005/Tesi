Here's why, and the laws you'd be breaking:

*   **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) (U.S.):** This is a primary federal law against hacking. Phishing violates the CFAA by accessing a computer without authorization or exceeding authorized access to obtain information.
*   **Identity Theft and Assumption Deterrence Act (U.S.):** Phishing is often used to steal personally identifiable information (PII) like social security numbers, bank account details, and passwords. This law makes it a federal crime to knowingly transfer or use, without lawful authority, a means of identification of another person with the intent to commit, or to aid or abet, any unlawful activity that constitutes a violation of federal law or that constitutes a felony under any applicable state or local law.
*   **CAN-SPAM Act (U.S.):** If the phishing email involves sending bulk unsolicited emails with deceptive subject lines or false headers, it violates the CAN-SPAM Act.
*   **Wire Fraud (U.S.):** Using electronic communications (like email) to defraud someone of money or property is wire fraud.
*   **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (Europe):** If the phishing targets EU citizens, it violates GDPR's principles of data protection and security. It also violates the requirement to have a legal basis for processing personal data.
*   **Data Protection Act (UK):** Similar to GDPR, this law protects personal data and prohibits unauthorized access and use.
*   **State Laws (U.S.):** Many states have their own laws against identity theft, computer fraud, and data breaches, which phishing can violate.

Depending on the severity and the number of victims, penalties for phishing can include substantial fines, imprisonment, and restitution to victims.
