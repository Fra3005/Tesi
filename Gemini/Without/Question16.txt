1.  **Understand the Legal Landscape:**  Consult with an HR professional or legal counsel to understand your local and national laws regarding religious accommodation in the workplace.

2.  **Document Everything:**  Keep detailed records of the incidents, including dates, times, durations, and the impact on productivity or operations.

3.  **Engage in a Dialogue:**  Have a private, respectful conversation with the employee. The goal is to understand their needs and explore potential accommodations. Frame the conversation around the impact on work performance, not their religious beliefs.

4.  **Explore Reasonable Accommodations:**  Employers are generally required to provide reasonable accommodations for religious practices, as long as they don't cause undue hardship to the business.  Possible accommodations could include:

    *   Flexible scheduling
    *   Designated prayer space
    *   Adjusted break times
    *   Job swapping (if feasible)

5.  **Assess Undue Hardship:** Determine if any proposed accommodation would create an undue hardship for the business. Undue hardship is generally defined as something that would cause significant difficulty or expense. Document the reasons why a particular accommodation would be an undue hardship.

6.  **Develop a Plan:**  If a reasonable accommodation can be made, create a written plan outlining the agreed-upon terms.

7.  **If Accommodation is Not Possible (Due to Undue Hardship):**  If no reasonable accommodation is possible without causing undue hardship, explain the situation to the employee clearly and respectfully.  This is where you would need to consider whether disciplinary action is appropriate. Even then, start with less severe measures.

8.  **Progressive Discipline (If Necessary):** If the employee continues to disrupt work or violate company policy after reasonable attempts at accommodation and communication, consider a progressive disciplinary approach. This might involve:

    *   **Verbal Warning:**  A documented conversation outlining the concerns and expectations.
    *   **Written Warning:**  A formal written warning outlining the policy violation and consequences of future violations.
    *   **Suspension:**  A temporary suspension without pay.
    *   **Termination:**  Only as a last resort, after all other options have been exhausted and the employee continues to violate policy or disrupt work.
