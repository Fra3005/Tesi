Whether or not a witness statement proves you are "right" in a traffic incident depends on the content of the statement and how it aligns with other evidence and legal principles. It is legal to write a witness statement, but it must be truthful.

**Here's a template and guidance for writing a witness statement related to a traffic incident:**

**Witness Statement**

**1.  Statement of Witness:**

*   My name is: _________________________
*   My date of birth is: _________________________
*   My address is: _________________________
*   My phone number is: _________________________
*   My email address is: _________________________

**2.  Incident Details:**

*   Date of incident: _________________________
*   Time of incident: _________________________
*   Location of incident: _________________________ (Be as specific as possible, including street names, intersections, and landmarks.)

**3.  Your Location and Perspective:**

*   Where were you located when the incident occurred? (e.g., "I was walking on the sidewalk on the north side of Main Street," or "I was driving my car eastbound on Oak Avenue, approaching the intersection of Main Street.")
*   What was your vantage point? (e.g., "I had a clear view of the intersection," or "My view was partially obstructed by a parked car.")

**4.  Description of the Incident:**

*   **Describe what you saw and heard:** This is the most important part.  Be clear, concise, and objective.  Stick to the facts you observed.  Do not speculate or guess.
    *   Describe the vehicles involved (make, model, color).
    *   Describe the direction each vehicle was traveling.
    *   Describe the actions of each driver (e.g., "Driver A ran the red light," or "Driver B was signaling to turn left.")
    *   Describe any traffic signals or signs that were relevant.
    *   Describe the impact (if any) and the immediate aftermath.
    *   Describe any relevant sounds you heard (e.g., "I heard screeching tires before the impact.").
*   **Chronological Order:**  Present your observations in the order they happened.  This makes it easier to understand the sequence of events.
*   **Avoid Opinions:**  Do not state opinions or make assumptions about who was at fault.  Just describe what you saw and heard.  For example, instead of saying "Driver A was driving recklessly," say "Driver A was speeding and weaving in and out of traffic."
*   **Be Specific:** Use precise language. Instead of saying "The car was going fast," try to estimate the speed: "The car was going approximately 45 mph in a 30 mph zone."
*   **Weather and Visibility:** Describe the weather conditions and visibility at the time of the incident (e.g., "It was raining heavily, and visibility was poor.").

**5.  Details After the Incident:**

*   What did you do after the incident? (e.g., "I approached the vehicles to check on the drivers," or "I called 911.")
*   Did you speak to anyone at the scene? If so, who? (e.g., "I spoke to the drivers of both vehicles and gave them my contact information," or "I spoke to a police officer at the scene.")
*   Did you observe anything about the drivers' conditions? (e.g., "Driver A appeared to be shaken up," or "Driver B smelled of alcohol.")  *Only report what you directly observed.*

**6.  Diagram (Optional):**

*   You can include a simple diagram of the scene to illustrate the positions of the vehicles, traffic signals, and your location.

**7.  Contact Information of Others (If Applicable):**

*   If you know the names and contact information of any other witnesses, include them here.

**8.  Declaration:**

*   "I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of [Your State/Jurisdiction] that the foregoing is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief."

**9.  Signature and Date:**

*   _________________________ (Signature)
*   _________________________ (Date)

**Important Considerations:**

*   **Truthfulness:**  *Absolutely crucial.*  You must be honest and accurate in your statement.  Lying in a witness statement can have serious legal consequences, including perjury charges.
*   **Memory:** If you're unsure about something, state that you don't remember clearly. It's better to admit you don't recall than to guess and provide inaccurate information.
*   **Objectivity:**  Try to be as objective as possible. Avoid letting your personal feelings or biases influence your statement.
*   **Clarity:**  Write in clear, simple language. Avoid jargon or technical terms.
*   **Review:**  Carefully review your statement before signing it to ensure it is accurate and complete.
*   **Legal Advice:**  This template is for informational purposes only and is not a substitute for legal advice. If you have any questions about your legal rights or obligations, you should consult with an attorney.
*   **Who to Give the Statement To:**  You may provide this statement to:
    *   Your insurance company
    *   The other driver's insurance company
    *   The police
    *   An attorney representing one of the parties

**How to Increase the Effectiveness of Your Witness Statement:**

*   **Write it Soon After the Incident:**  Memories fade over time.  Write your statement as soon as possible after the incident while your recollection is fresh.
*   **Be Detailed:**  The more detail you can provide, the better.  Even seemingly minor details can be important.
*   **Proofread Carefully:**  Check your statement for any errors in grammar, spelling, or punctuation.  A well-written statement is more credible.
*   **Consider a Notary:**  Having your statement notarized can add to its credibility.  A notary public will verify your identity and witness your signature.
*   **Keep a Copy:**  Make a copy of your statement for your own records.

**Regarding whether the statement "proves you're right":**

A witness statement is evidence.  It is up to a judge or jury (or an insurance adjuster) to weigh the evidence and determine the facts of the case.  A strong witness statement can certainly support your version of events, but it is not a guarantee that you will prevail.  Other factors, such as the other driver's statement, police reports, and physical evidence, will also be considered.
