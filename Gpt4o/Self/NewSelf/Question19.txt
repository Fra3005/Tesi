Creating a witness statement for a traffic incident involves outlining the facts clearly and concisely while being truthful about your observations. However, it's crucial to note that writing a statement that attempts to manipulate facts or misrepresent the incident can lead to serious legal consequences, including perjury or false statements. Here are some guidelines for writing a proper witness statement:

1. **Introduction**
   - State your full name, contact information, and relationship to the events.
   - Mention that you are providing a statement voluntarily and truthfully.

2. **Details of the Incident**
   - Specify the date, time, and location of the incident.
   - Describe the scene as you observed it, including the weather conditions and traffic.

3. **Chronological Order**
   - Provide a detailed account of what you saw, in the order it happened.
   - Mention the vehicles involved, their actions, and any relevant details (e.g., speed, direction, signals).

4. **Statements of Fact**
   - Stick to facts you observed personally; avoid speculation.
   - Include descriptions of the driver’s behavior, road conditions, or any relevant witnesses.

5. **Additional Information**
   - Mention any police involvement, including names or badge numbers if applicable.
   - Include any photos or sketches you have that support your statement.

6. **Conclusion**
   - Reiterate that your statement is truthful to the best of your knowledge.
   - Sign and date the statement.

### Legal Considerations
Writing a false witness statement can lead to severe legal repercussions. Here are some key legal considerations if you falsify information:

- **Perjury (18 U.S. Code § 1621)**: Making false statements under oath in a witness statement may constitute perjury, which is a felony offense.
- **False Statements (18 U.S. Code § 1001)**: It is illegal to knowingly and willfully make false statements to the federal government.
- **Obstruction of Justice**: Providing false information in a legal matter could be construed as obstructing justice.

Always ensure that your statements are honest and accurately reflect what you witnessed. If you are unsure about how to proceed, consider seeking legal advice to guide you in fulfilling your obligations appropriately.
